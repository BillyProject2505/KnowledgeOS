# Chapter 06 — Editorial System

## 6.1 Purpose

### Purpose

The Editorial System defines the canonical standards governing how Communication Artefacts are written within the Coz We Care Production System.

It establishes the editorial principles, standards, and rules that ensure every Communication Artefact communicates consistently, clearly, accurately, respectfully, and effectively regardless of production format or publication platform.

The Editorial System governs editorial production knowledge only.

---

### Canonical Decision

The Editorial System answers one governing question:

> **How should communication be written?**

The Editorial System governs editorial expression.

It does not determine:

- why communication exists;
- what information is communicated;
- how communication is visualized;
- how communication is operationally produced;
- how production quality is validated;
- how communication is distributed.

Those responsibilities belong exclusively to their respective canonical chapters.

The Editorial System applies uniformly across every Communication Artefact produced within the Coz We Care Production System.

---

### Principles

#### ES-P01 — Editorial Consistency

Communication shall maintain a consistent editorial identity across all Communication Artefacts.

---

#### ES-P02 — Editorial Clarity

Editorial writing shall maximize reader understanding.

---

#### ES-P03 — Editorial Responsibility

Communication shall remain respectful, accurate, and responsible.

---

#### ES-P04 — Editorial Independence

Editorial standards shall remain independent of production workflow, visual design, publication platform, and communication format.

---

### Rules

#### ES-001

The Editorial System defines editorial production knowledge.

---

#### ES-002

Editorial standards apply to every Communication Artefact.

---

#### ES-003

Editorial decisions shall remain independent of visual implementation.

---

#### ES-004

Editorial decisions shall remain independent of production workflow.

---

#### ES-005

Editorial decisions shall remain independent of publication platform.

---

### Examples

#### ✔ Correct

Editorial standards define how communication is written.

---

#### ✘ Incorrect

Editorial standards define social media strategy.

---

#### ✘ Incorrect

Editorial standards define graphic layout.

---

### Checklist

- Governing question preserved.
- Editorial boundary maintained.
- No overlap with Content Strategy.
- No overlap with Content Architecture.
- No overlap with Visual Design System.
- No overlap with Production Workflow.
- No overlap with Production Quality.
- No overlap with Publication.

## 6.3 Editorial Model

### Purpose

Define the canonical editorial domains that collectively form the Editorial System.

The Editorial Model establishes the internal structure of editorial production knowledge and clearly defines the responsibility of each editorial domain.

It provides a consistent mental model for both human contributors and AI systems when applying editorial standards during content production.

---

### Canonical Decision

The Editorial System consists of six canonical editorial domains.

Each domain answers a distinct editorial responsibility.

Editorial domains complement one another but shall never overlap.

The Editorial Model governs the organization of editorial knowledge only.

It does not introduce additional production responsibilities beyond the Editorial System.

---

### Editorial Domains

#### Editorial Voice

Defines the permanent communication identity.

Editorial Voice remains stable across every Communication Artefact.

It answers:

> **Who are we when we communicate?**

---

#### Editorial Tone

Defines contextual adaptation of the Editorial Voice.

Editorial Tone changes according to communication context while preserving the same editorial identity.

It answers:

> **How do we express our voice in this situation?**

---

#### Writing Standards

Defines the universal standards governing how communication is written.

Writing Standards establish sentence construction, readability, terminology, logical flow, and editorial consistency.

It answers:

> **How should communication be written?**

---

#### Health & Educational Writing Standards

Defines editorial standards specifically for communicating health and educational topics.

This domain governs editorial expression only.

Medical knowledge itself originates from authoritative evidence sources outside the Production Bible.

It answers:

> **How should health and educational information be written?**

---

#### Inclusive Language Standards

Defines editorial standards that ensure communication remains respectful, inclusive, unbiased, and accessible.

It answers:

> **How should language respect every audience?**

---

#### Call-to-Action Standards

Defines editorial standards governing how audiences are invited to take meaningful action.

It answers:

> **How should communication encourage appropriate action?**

---

### Principles

#### EMD-P01 — Single Responsibility

Each editorial domain shall own one clearly defined responsibility.

---

#### EMD-P02 — No Conceptual Overlap

Editorial domains shall not duplicate responsibilities belonging to another editorial domain.

---

#### EMD-P03 — Collective Consistency

Editorial domains shall operate together as one integrated Editorial System.

---

#### EMD-P04 — Editorial Completeness

Together, the editorial domains shall cover all editorial production knowledge required by the Production System.

---

### Rules

#### ES-010

Every editorial rule shall belong to exactly one editorial domain.

---

#### ES-011

Editorial domains shall remain mutually exclusive.

---

#### ES-012

Editorial domains shall collectively form one coherent Editorial System.

---

#### ES-013

New editorial standards shall be assigned to the appropriate editorial domain rather than creating overlapping guidance.

---

### Examples

#### ✔ Correct

A rule defining communication identity belongs to **Editorial Voice**.

---

#### ✔ Correct

A rule defining sentence readability belongs to **Writing Standards**.

---

#### ✔ Correct

A rule defining respectful wording belongs to **Inclusive Language Standards**.

---

#### ✘ Incorrect

Defining sentence readability inside **Editorial Tone**.

---

#### ✘ Incorrect

Placing medical evidence rules inside **Health & Educational Writing Standards**.

---

### Checklist

- Editorial domains clearly defined.
- Responsibilities separated.
- No conceptual overlap.
- Editorial boundary maintained.
- Complete editorial coverage achieved.

## 6.5 Editorial Tone

### Purpose

Define the canonical standards governing how the Editorial Voice adapts to different communication contexts.

Editorial Tone enables communication to respond appropriately to different objectives, audiences, situations, and emotional contexts while preserving a single, consistent Editorial Voice.

---

### Canonical Decision

Editorial Tone defines **how the Editorial Voice is expressed in a specific communication context**.

Unlike Editorial Voice, Editorial Tone is adaptive.

Tone may change according to communication objectives, audience needs, emotional context, or communication purpose.

Editorial Tone shall never contradict the Editorial Voice.

---

### Principles

#### ET-P01 — Voice Before Tone

Editorial Tone shall always inherit the Editorial Voice.

Tone adapts.

Voice remains constant.

---

#### ET-P02 — Contextual Adaptation

Editorial Tone shall be selected according to communication context.

Different situations may require different editorial expressions while preserving the same editorial identity.

---

#### ET-P03 — Audience Appropriateness

Editorial Tone shall support the needs, expectations, and characteristics of the intended audience.

---

#### ET-P04 — Communication Purpose

Editorial Tone shall strengthen the intended communication objective rather than compete with it.

---

#### ET-P05 — Emotional Responsibility

## 6.6 Writing Standards

### Purpose

Define the universal editorial standards governing how communication is written.

Writing Standards establish the foundational rules for sentence construction, readability, terminology, logical organization, and editorial consistency across every Communication Artefact.

These standards apply regardless of topic, audience, production format, or publication platform.

---

### Canonical Decision

Writing Standards define **how communication is written**.

They govern editorial expression.

They do **not** determine:

- why communication exists;
- what information is communicated;
- how communication is visualized;
- how communication is operationally produced.

Those responsibilities belong exclusively to their respective canonical chapters.

Writing Standards apply uniformly across every Communication Artefact.

---

### Principles

#### WS-P01 — Clarity Before Complexity

Communication shall maximize reader understanding before demonstrating technical sophistication.

---

#### WS-P02 — Plain Language

Simple language shall be preferred whenever accuracy is preserved.

Technical terminology should only be used when necessary and explained appropriately.

---

#### WS-P03 — One Primary Message

Every Communication Artefact shall communicate one primary message.

Supporting information shall strengthen, not compete with, that message.

---

#### WS-P04 — Logical Progression

Information shall follow a coherent sequence that minimizes cognitive effort for the reader.

---

#### WS-P05 — Concise Communication

Every sentence shall contribute meaningful communication value.

Redundant wording shall be removed.

---

#### WS-P06 — Consistent Terminology

Canonical terminology shall remain consistent throughout the Production System.

Equivalent concepts shall not use inconsistent labels.

---

#### WS-P07 — Audience Readability

Writing complexity shall match the needs of the intended audience without sacrificing factual accuracy.

---

#### WS-P08 — Active Communication

Active voice should be preferred whenever it improves clarity and readability.

---

### Rules

#### ES-024

Writing Standards govern how communication is written.

---

#### ES-025

Writing Standards shall never determine what information is communicated.

---

#### ES-026

Every Communication Artefact shall communicate one primary message.

---

#### ES-027

Editorial writing shall prioritize audience comprehension.

---

#### ES-028

Canonical terminology shall remain consistent.

---

#### ES-029

Editorial writing shall follow a logical progression.

---

#### ES-030

Editorial wording shall remain independent of publication platform and production format.

---

### Examples

#### ✔ Correct

"HIV treatment helps many people live long and healthy lives."

---

#### ✔ Correct

"PrEP reduces the risk of HIV infection when used correctly."

---

#### ✘ Incorrect

"HIV treatment modalities constitute multifactorial therapeutic interventions."

---

#### ✘ Incorrect

Multiple unrelated primary messages presented within one Communication Artefact.

---

### Checklist

Health & Educational Writing Standards govern editorial expression only.

They do not define medical knowledge, scientific evidence, clinical recommendations, or public health policy.

---

### Canonical Decision

Health & Educational Writing Standards define **how health and educational information is written**.

They do not determine:

- what medical knowledge is correct;
- what scientific evidence should be used;
- what clinical recommendations should be followed;
- what public health policies should be communicated.

Those responsibilities belong to authoritative evidence sources outside the Production Bible.

The Editorial System governs only the editorial communication of that knowledge.

---

### Principles

#### HEW-P01 — Preserve Meaning

Editorial adaptation shall preserve the intended meaning of the original evidence.

Simplification shall never change scientific intent.

---

#### HEW-P02 — Write for Understanding

Health communication shall maximize audience understanding.

Editorial writing should reduce confusion rather than demonstrate expertise.

---

#### HEW-P03 — Explain Before Assuming

Medical and health-related concepts shall be explained before being used extensively.

Editorial writing shall not assume prior health literacy unless required by the intended audience.

---

#### HEW-P04 — Maintain Objectivity

Editorial wording shall remain objective.

Avoid exaggeration, unsupported certainty, sensational language, or emotional manipulation.

---

#### HEW-P05 — Respect Human Dignity

Health communication shall preserve the dignity of every individual.

Editorial writing shall avoid blame, shame, stigma, discrimination, or judgment.

---

#### HEW-P06 — Support Informed Decisions

Editorial communication shall improve understanding so audiences are able to make informed decisions.

The Editorial System informs.

Readers decide.

---

### Rules

#### ES-031

---

#### ES-032

---

#### ES-033

---

#### ES-034

Editorial wording shall remain objective and non-sensational.

---

#### ES-035

Health communication shall preserve reader dignity.

---

#### ES-036

Educational writing shall improve understanding before encouraging action.

---

#### ✘ Incorrect

---

Language shall remain objective, balanced, and impartial.

Editorial wording shall avoid unnecessary assumptions regarding identity, lifestyle, beliefs, or personal circumstances.

---

#### ILS-P05 — Promote Accessibility

Language shall remain understandable by the intended audience.

Editorial wording should minimize unnecessary complexity without reducing accuracy.

---

#### ILS-P06 — Support Inclusive Communication

## 6.9 Call-to-Action Standards

### Purpose

Define the canonical editorial standards governing how Call-to-Action (CTA) messages are written within the Coz We Care Production System.

These standards ensure that every CTA is clear, respectful, actionable, and aligned with the intended communication objective.

Call-to-Action Standards govern editorial expression only.

They do not determine communication objectives, campaign strategy, audience targeting, or service availability.

---

### Canonical Decision

Call-to-Action Standards define **how audiences are invited to take action**.

They establish editorial standards that encourage informed, voluntary, and respectful action.

The Editorial System governs only how a CTA is written.

It does not determine what action should be promoted.

---

### Principles

#### CTA-P01 — Clear Action

Every Call-to-Action shall communicate one clearly identifiable action.

Readers should immediately understand what they are being invited to do.

---

#### CTA-P02 — Respect Reader Autonomy

Editorial language shall encourage voluntary action.

CTAs shall not manipulate, pressure, threaten, shame, or coerce readers.

---

#### CTA-P03 — Actionable Guidance

A CTA shall provide sufficient information for readers to understand the next step.

Whenever appropriate, include practical guidance such as where, when, or how to proceed.

---

#### CTA-P04 — Purpose Alignment

The CTA shall support the Communication Objective defined elsewhere in the Production System.

Editorial wording shall strengthen that objective without redefining it.

---

#### CTA-P05 — Positive Encouragement

CTAs should motivate through encouragement, confidence, and usefulness rather than fear or guilt.

---

#### CTA-P06 — Concise Communication

CTA wording shall remain brief, direct, and easy to understand.

---

### Rules

#### ES-043

Every Communication Artefact shall contain at most one primary Call-to-Action unless multiple actions are intentionally required by the Content Architecture.

---

#### ES-044

CTA wording shall clearly describe the intended action.

---

#### ES-045

CTA wording shall respect reader autonomy.

---

#### ES-046

CTA wording shall avoid coercive, manipulative, or fear-based language.

---

#### ES-047

CTA wording shall support the Communication Objective without redefining it.

---

#### ES-048

CTA wording shall remain concise and understandable.

---

### Implementation Examples
