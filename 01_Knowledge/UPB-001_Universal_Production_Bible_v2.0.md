# Core Architectural Baseline

## 1. Universal Production Knowledge

The Universal Production Bible (UPB) is the canonical specification and
architecture of universal production knowledge.

The UPB establishes the common foundation from which applicable
Production Bible Systems inherit, apply, specialize, validate, and
evolve production knowledge.

The UPB shall contain only knowledge that is genuinely universal within
its intended production scope.

## 2. Universal / Project Relationship

``` text
Universal Production Knowledge
            │
            ▼
Production Bible System
            │
            ▼
Project-Specific Production Knowledge
```

Inheritance shall preserve the canonical meaning and constraints of
universal knowledge while permitting authorized project-specific
extension.

## 3. Implementation Independence

Universal production knowledge shall remain independent of:

-   individual projects;
-   media;
-   technologies;
-   implementation methods;
-   repositories;
-   document formats.

## 4. Canonical Integrity

Every universal concept shall have one authoritative canonical home.

Project-specific knowledge shall extend universal knowledge without
redefining its canonical meaning.

## 01.1 Canonical Definition

The Universal Production Bible (UPB) is the canonical specification of
universal production knowledge.

## 01.2 Purpose

The UPB establishes and maintains the universal production knowledge
required to create a consistent, reusable, scalable, and continuously
improving foundation for Production Bible Systems.

## 01.3 Vision

To establish a shared foundation of universal production knowledge that
enables consistent, reusable, scalable, and continuously improving
production across applicable production systems.

## 01.4 Mission

To define, preserve, organise, and evolve universal production knowledge
independently of projects, media, technologies, and implementation
methods.

## 01.5 Canonical Function

The UPB shall:

1.  define universal production knowledge;
2.  establish architectural relationships among universal production
    knowledge domains;
3.  establish boundaries within which universal production knowledge may
    be inherited and applied;
4.  provide the universal foundation from which Production Bible Systems
    may be constructed;
5.  preserve universal knowledge independently of project-specific
    implementation;
6.  enable universal knowledge to evolve through validated production
    experience.

## 01.6 Architectural Role

The UPB occupies the **Universal Production Knowledge Architecture
Layer**.

It is:

-   upstream of Production Bible Systems;
-   independent of individual production implementations;
-   reusable across applicable production contexts;
-   extensible through controlled inheritance;
-   authoritative for knowledge explicitly defined as universal.

The UPB is not a project Production Bible, production implementation,
operational system, document-management system, or document-formatting
standard.

## 01.7 Scope

The UPB includes universal production knowledge concerning:

-   production intent and strategy;
-   production requirements and context;
-   production knowledge and its semantic structure;
-   knowledge relationships and contextual applicability;
-   evidence and provenance;
-   production resources and capabilities;
-   production activities, decisions, and workflows;
-   production outputs;
-   state, validation, approval, and transition;
-   change, learning, and evolution;
-   governance of canonical production knowledge;
-   publication authority for canonical production knowledge.

## 01.8 Boundary

The UPB shall not contain:

-   project-specific production implementation;
-   project-specific brand systems;
-   project-specific audience definitions;
-   project-specific editorial standards;
-   project-specific visual standards;
-   operational records;
-   project-management procedures;
-   implementation technologies;
-   repository-specific implementation;
-   document-formatting requirements;
-   Universal Document Standard content.

The UPB shall not assume canonical ownership of knowledge whose
authoritative home belongs to another system.

### CD-01-005 --- Canonical Integrity

## 01.10 Constitutional Principles

## 02.1 Function

Chapter 02 defines the minimum universal principles, rules, constraints,
and decision orientation that govern interpretation and use of
production knowledge throughout the UPB.

## 02.2 Foundational Principles

### FP-001 --- Foundation First

Foundational constraints shall be established and understood before
downstream production knowledge is applied.

### FP-002 --- One Concept, One Home

Every canonical concept shall have one authoritative home.

### FP-003 --- Consistency Over Convenience

Canonical consistency shall take precedence over local convenience.

### FP-004 --- Evidence Before Assumption

Evidence shall be established before an assumption is treated as a basis
for canonical production knowledge or decision-making.

### FP-005 --- Reuse Before Creation

Existing applicable canonical knowledge shall be reused before creating
new knowledge.

### FP-006 --- Simplicity Before Complexity

The minimum sufficient architecture shall be preferred over unnecessary
structural complexity.

### FP-007 --- Traceable Decisions

Material canonical decisions shall remain traceable to their basis,
authority, and resulting state where applicable.

### FP-008 --- Implementation Independence

Universal knowledge shall describe production meaning and constraints
without becoming dependent on a particular implementation.

### FP-009 --- Controlled Inheritance

Project-specific knowledge may specialize or extend universal knowledge
only within authorized boundaries and without silently redefining
universal meaning.

### FP-010 --- Continuous Improvement

Validated production learning may improve universal knowledge through
controlled canonical evolution.

## 02.3 Constraints

1.  Canonical knowledge shall not be duplicated merely for convenience.
2.  Unresolved assumptions shall not be silently promoted to canonical
    knowledge.
3.  Project-specific knowledge shall not be promoted to universal
    knowledge without a universalization decision.
4.  Architectural ownership shall remain distinct from implementation
    ownership.
5.  A registry shall not become the substantive owner of knowledge
    merely by registering it.
6.  A document shall not become the semantic authority merely by
    containing canonical text.
7.  Quality requirements shall not be conflated with lifecycle state.
8.  Classification shall not be conflated with applicability.
9.  Object Type shall remain distinct from Classification.
10. Universal architecture shall remain implementation-independent.

## 02.4 Evidence Before Assumption

When evidence is incomplete:

1.  identify the evidence gap;
2.  distinguish observed facts from inference;
3.  avoid inventing canonical meaning;
4.  record the unresolved condition where traceability requires it;
5.  resolve only through an authorized canonical decision.

## 02.5 Architectural Minimality

UPB architecture shall introduce a new taxonomy, object class, chapter,
registry, or architectural layer only when an existing canonical home
cannot satisfy the requirement without violating an established
boundary.

## 02.6 Universalization Boundary

A candidate knowledge item may enter universal architecture only when
its universality is demonstrated against:

-   cross-project applicability;
-   implementation independence;
-   semantic stability;
-   non-duplication;
-   architectural fit;
-   production value.

## 02.7 Quality Boundary

Quality responsibilities are divided:

``` text
        ↓
validate production readiness / conformance

State & Evolution Validation
        ↓
validate state, transition, evolution, and change integrity
```

Neither replaces the other.

## 02.8 AI Consumption Principle

UPB knowledge shall be optimized primarily for AI consumption.

Canonical documentation shall prioritize:

-   deterministic interpretation;
-   semantic locality;
-   explicit boundaries;
-   explicit relationships;

## 03.1 Purpose & Architectural Role

Knowledge Architecture defines the universal semantic architecture of
production knowledge.

It is the **Universal Knowledge Semantic Architecture Layer**.

It establishes how production knowledge is represented as canonical,
identifiable, referenceable, maintainable, and bounded Production
Knowledge Objects.

## 03.2 Production Knowledge Object

A **Production Knowledge Object (PKO)** is a canonical, reusable,
independently maintainable, and independently referenceable unit of
production knowledge representing one canonical production concept.

### KA-001 --- One Concept, One Object

One canonical production concept shall have one canonical Production
Knowledge Object unless an explicitly governed architectural reason
establishes otherwise.

A PKO may be:

-   universal;
-   domain-specific;
-   project-specific.

The object model is universal even when the object itself is not.

## 03.3 Object Identity

A PKO identity consists of:

-   Object ID;
-   Object Name;
-   Object Type.

Identity shall be:

-   unique;
-   stable;
-   permanent;
-   independently referenceable;
-   independent of version;
-   independent of filename;
-   independent of repository location.

### KA-002 --- Identity Belongs to Knowledge

Object identity belongs to the knowledge object, not to its
representation.

Object identifiers shall not be reassigned to another object.

## 03.4 Canonical Knowledge Structure

``` text
Production Knowledge Object
│
├── Identity
├── Object Type
├── Canonical Content
├── Classification
├── Applicability Information
├── Canonical Source
├── Canonical Location
├── Relationships
└── Metadata
```

### Canonical Content

Canonical Content contains the substantive knowledge represented by the
PKO.

### KA-004 --- Content Before Metadata

Substantive canonical knowledge shall remain distinct from descriptive
metadata.

### Metadata

Metadata describes the object for purposes including:

-   classification;
-   applicability;
-   referenceability;
-   governance;
-   lifecycle;
-   version;
-   traceability.

### KA-005 --- Metadata Does Not Transfer Ownership

Metadata may reference another architectural domain without transferring
semantic ownership of that domain to Chapter 03.

## 03.5 Object Type

Object Type identifies the fundamental form of knowledge represented by
a PKO.

Canonical taxonomy:

``` text
Philosophy
Principle
Model
Classification
Objective
Standard
System
```

Object Type shall not encode:

-   domain;
-   applicability;
-   project;
-   lifecycle;
-   version;
-   repository;
-   registry state.

`Relationship` is not an Object Type.

## 03.6 Classification & Applicability

### Classification

Classification identifies the knowledge domain to which the PKO belongs.

Current canonical domain model:

``` text
Foundation
Brand
Audience
Strategic
Content
Editorial
Visual
```

A PKO has one primary domain where the applicable classification model
requires one.

Cross-domain influence shall be represented through relationships rather
than default multi-domain ownership.

### Applicability Information

Applicability identifies where, when, for whom, or under what context a
PKO applies.

It may describe:

-   production scope;
-   project context;
-   domain context;
-   audience context;
-   other authorized applicability dimensions.

### KA-006 --- Applicability Is Not Classification

Applicability information shall not be silently promoted into
Classification taxonomy.

No independent universal Scope taxonomy is established by Chapter 03.

## 03.7 Canonical Source & Canonical Location

### Canonical Source

Identifies the authoritative source, origin, or reference through which
knowledge may be verified where applicable.

### Canonical Location

Identifies where the authoritative representation of the PKO is
maintained within the canonical production-knowledge architecture.

### KA-007 --- Source Is Not Location

``` text
Canonical Source
≠
Canonical Location
```

Relocation of the canonical representation shall not change object
identity.

## 03.8 Object / Representation Boundary

``` text
Knowledge Object
    ≠ Document
    ≠ File
    ≠ Repository
    ≠ Registry Record
    ≠ Artifact
```

### KA-003 --- Object Before Representation

Knowledge architecture shall be defined independently of document or
storage representation.

### KA-009 --- Representation Is Not Identity

A document, file, repository path, artifact, or registry record shall
not substitute for canonical object identity.

### KA-008 --- Registry Is Not Knowledge

A registry records or indexes canonical knowledge; it does not replace
the substantive knowledge object.

## 03.9 Referenceability & Relationships

Every PKO shall be independently referenceable through canonical
identity.

Reference shall not depend solely on:

-   document position;
-   chapter number;
-   filename;
-   repository path;
-   textual similarity.

PKOs may participate in material relationships.

Chapter 03 establishes the structural existence of relationships;
relationship semantics and vocabulary belong to the Knowledge
Relationship Architecture.

## 03.10 Lifecycle & Version Boundary

Chapter 03 does not own:

-   lifecycle definitions;
-   state transitions;
-   approval;
-   release;
-   supersession;
-   withdrawal;
-   evolution;
-   version-control semantics.

Lifecycle and version are not object identity.

## 03.11 Authority & Ownership Boundary

The following remain distinct:

``` text

Ownership
Authorship
Repository
Registry
```

Chapter 03 may represent applicable metadata concerning these concepts
without becoming the semantic owner of their broader architectures.

## 03.12 Universal Model & Knowledge Instances

The Production Knowledge Object model is universal.

Individual PKOs may represent:

-   universal knowledge;
-   domain-specific knowledge;
-   project-specific knowledge.

Universal object architecture does not imply universal applicability of

## 03.13 Conformance Conditions

1.  one canonical production concept is identifiable;

## 05.04 Evidence Definition

**Evidence** is information that provides a basis for supporting, evaluating, verifying, or challenging a production knowledge claim, interpretation, or canonical decision.

Evidence shall remain distinguishable from the conclusion it supports.

```text
Evidence
    ↓ supports
Claim / Interpretation / Decision
```

## 05.05 Evidence Before Assumption

Evidence shall be established before an assumption is treated as a basis for canonical production knowledge or decision-making.

When evidence is incomplete:
1. identify the evidence gap;
2. distinguish observed facts from inference;
3. avoid inventing canonical meaning;
4. preserve unresolved conditions where materially relevant;
5. resolve only through an authorized canonical decision.

Chapter 05 owns the epistemic distinction; governance owns authorization of final decisions.

## 05.06 Observed Fact vs Inference

The architecture shall preserve:

```text
Observed / evidenced
        ≠
Inferred
        ≠
Assumed
        ≠
Canonical
```

An inference may be reasonable without becoming an observed fact. An assumption may be operationally useful without becoming canonical knowledge.

## 05.07 Evidence Gap

An **Evidence Gap** exists when available evidence is insufficient to establish or verify the intended production claim or decision to the required degree.

Absence of evidence shall not be silently converted into evidence for a conclusion.

## 05.08 Source vs Evidence

```text
Source
    ↓ may provide
Evidence
    ↓ may support
Knowledge / Decision
```

Therefore:

> **Source ≠ Evidence**

A source may contain evidence, but a source reference does not automatically establish evidentiary sufficiency.

## 05.09 Provenance

**Provenance** records the origin, basis, derivation, or historical path relevant to understanding how knowledge, evidence, or a decision came to exist.

It may reference:
- originating source;
- supporting evidence;
- originating decision;
- contributing knowledge;
- historical context;
- transformation or derivation;
- conflict and resolution basis.

## 05.10 Provenance ≠ Canonicality

```text
Clear provenance
    ≠
Canonical status
```

Provenance establishes origin/basis. Canonicality establishes canonical status.

## 05.11 Provenance ≠ Authority

```text
Provenance
→ where / how did this originate?

Authority
→ who or what establishes canonical legitimacy?
```

Governance Architecture owns authority semantics.

## 05.12 Evidence ≠ Decision

Evidence may support a decision but does not constitute the decision.

## 05.13 Evidence ≠ Quality

Evidence provides epistemic basis. Quality Architecture evaluates production outputs or conformance against applicable requirements.

## 05.14 Evidence ≠ Lifecycle

Evidence may explain why a state transition or change occurred, but it does not define lifecycle state.

## 05.15 Evidence Support

Where evidence materially supports a knowledge object or canonical decision, that support shall be traceable.

```text
Evidence
    ↓ supports
Knowledge / Decision
```

## 05.16 Evidence Conflict

When evidence sources conflict:
1. preserve the conflicting evidence;
2. identify relevant source/provenance context;
3. distinguish the conflict from the conclusion;
4. avoid silently merging incompatible evidence;
5. identify the authorized resolution where one exists;
6. preserve unresolved conflict where evidence is insufficient.


## 07.12 Resource Purpose

Every materially used production resource should have an identifiable production purpose where material.

Purpose may describe:
- production activity supported;
- output supported;
- requirement satisfied;
- reusable production function enabled.

## 07.13 Resource Applicability

A resource may be applicable only to particular production contexts, output types, domains, capabilities, audiences, or environments.

```text
Resource Exists
    ≠
Resource Applies Everywhere
```

## 07.14 Resource Reusability

Production resources should be reusable where their nature permits.

Reusability does not imply universal applicability.

## 07.15 Resource Accessibility

Production resources should be accessible to authorized production activities and agents when required.

Accessibility is a semantic requirement and does not prescribe storage technology, access-control platform, search engine, repository, or API.

## 07.16 Resource Traceability

Material production resource use should be traceable where required.

```text
Production Output
       ↓ uses
Production Resource
```

The underlying relationship semantics remain governed by Chapter 04.

## 07.17 Resource Maintenance

Production resources should remain suitable for intended use through controlled maintenance.

Maintenance may address:
- outdated content;
- broken representations;
- obsolete variants;
- inconsistency;
- applicability changes;
- integrity problems.

Maintenance is not equivalent to lifecycle architecture.

## 07.18 Resource Integrity

A production resource shall preserve the properties required for its intended use.

Where a resource is canonical, its integrity shall remain consistent with the applicable canonical specification.

## 07.19 Resource Canonicality

A production resource may have canonical status where explicitly governed.

```text
Canonical Resource
      ≠
Canonical Knowledge Object
```

Chapter 07 recognizes canonicality as an applicable property; governance establishes canonical authority.

## 07.20 Resource Provenance

A resource may have provenance where its origin or derivation is materially relevant.

```text
Resource
   ↓ provenance
Source / Origin / Derivation
```

Chapter 05 owns provenance semantics.

## 07.21 Resource Ownership Boundary

Operational resource ownership may be required.

```text
Resource Ownership
      ≠
Governance Authority
```

Ownership information may identify maintenance responsibility without automatically granting canonical decision authority.

## 07.22 Resource Lifecycle Boundary

Resources may evolve, be superseded, withdrawn, or retired.

Chapter 07 defines resource-specific requirements around usability and maintenance. State & Evolution owns universal lifecycle semantics.

## 07.23 Resource vs Capability

```text
Capability
→ ability to perform

Resource
→ thing that enables/supports performance
```

## 07.24 Resource vs Production Activity

A production activity consumes or uses resources.

```text
Resource
   ↓ supports
Production Activity
```

Chapter 07 defines resource semantics; Production Architecture defines production activities.

## 07.25 Resource vs Quality

```text
Resource Architecture
→ defines resource

Quality Architecture
→ evaluates production output
```

Quality does not own resource semantics.

## 07.26 Resource vs Governance

Governance may determine whether a resource is canonical, who may authorize its use, whether it should be retired, and who is responsible for maintenance.

Chapter 07 does not own those authority decisions.

## 07.27 Resource Categories

The architecture intentionally does not establish mandatory universal classes such as:

```text
Asset
Reference
Template
Media
Component
Preset
File
```

These may be contextual categories.

The minimum universal abstraction is:

> **Production Resource**

```text
        ↓
```

## 07.29 Resource Representation

A Production Resource may have multiple representations:

```text
Production Resource
       ├── Representation A
       ├── Representation B
       └── Representation C
```

Changing representation does not necessarily create a new resource. Different representations may represent different resources if semantic identity differs.

## 07.30 Resource Reuse Boundary

Reuse should preserve:
- intended purpose;
- applicability;
- integrity;
- required provenance;
- required canonical status.

A resource shall not be reused merely because it is available if its applicability is not established.

## 07.31 Resource Duplication

Where an existing canonical or reusable resource satisfies the production requirement, unnecessary duplication should be avoided.

Duplication may be legitimate where semantic identity, applicability, or representation requirements differ.

## 07.32 Resource Accessibility vs Retrieval

```text
Resource
→ should be available when legitimately required

Retrieval
→ query / search / rank / retrieve / present
```

Only the first belongs to Chapter 07.

## 07.33 Resource Maintenance vs Lifecycle

```text
Maintenance
→ keep resource usable/current

Lifecycle
→ define state/evolution
```

Therefore:

```text
Maintenance
≠
Lifecycle
```

## 07.34 Resource Change

A material resource change may require maintenance, quality evaluation, lifecycle transition, or governance decision depending on context.

Chapter 07 does not independently authorize the change.

## 07.35 AI Consumption

AI consuming production resources should:
- identify applicable resources before production;
- prefer existing canonical/reusable resources where applicable;
- preserve required resource traceability;
- avoid unnecessary duplication;
- recognize resource applicability;
- avoid using outdated or inconsistent resources where current resources are required;
- identify resource gaps rather than inventing resource identity.

AI should not assume that a file's existence makes it canonical, current, applicable, or authoritative.

## 07.36 Resource Gap

A **Resource Gap** exists when a required production resource is not available, accessible, applicable, or sufficiently suitable for the intended production purpose.

```text
Required Resource
       ↓
Unavailable / Insufficient
       ↓
Resource Gap
       ↓
Governed resolution
```

## 07.37 Resource Conflict

Where multiple resources appear to satisfy the same production need but materially conflict, the conflict should remain identifiable until resolved through applicable authority.

Chapter 07 does not itself determine which conflicting resource is canonical.

## 07.38 Resource Quality

Resource quality and production-output quality remain distinct.

```text
Resource Integrity
→ suitability of resource

Production Quality
→ quality of output using resource
```

`Production Resource` remains the semantic abstraction.

## 07.41 Conformance Conditions

Production Resource Architecture conforms when:
1. production resources have identifiable semantic purpose where material;
2. resource applicability is distinguishable from availability;
3. reusable resources can be distinguished from one-off production outputs;
4. resource semantics remain distinct from knowledge semantics;
5. resource semantics remain distinct from evidence semantics;
6. resource semantics remain distinct from source and location;
7. resource representation remains distinct from resource identity;
8. material resource use can be traced where required;
9. resource maintenance requirements can be represented;
10. resource accessibility requirements can be represented;
11. canonical resource status is not assumed without governance;
12. lifecycle semantics remain under State & Evolution;
13. retrieval mechanisms remain outside resource semantics;
14. implementation technology does not define resource identity;
15. deferred resource taxonomies are not treated as universal requirements.

## 07.44 Canonical Resource Rules

### PR-001 — Resource Purpose
A materially used Production Resource shall have an identifiable production purpose.

### PR-002 — Resource Applicability
Resource applicability shall not be assumed merely from resource availability.

### PR-003 — Reuse Before Duplication
Where an applicable reusable resource exists, unnecessary duplication should be avoided.

### PR-004 — Preserve Resource Integrity
Resources shall remain suitable for their intended production use.

### PR-005 — Resource Traceability
Material resource use shall remain traceable where required.

### PR-006 — Resource Accessibility
Required resources shall be accessible to authorized production activities and agents where applicable.

### PR-007 — Resource Maintenance
Resources shall be maintained where continued suitability requires maintenance.

### PR-008 — Resource Is Not Knowledge
A resource representation shall not automatically be treated as canonical knowledge.

### PR-009 — Resource Is Not Evidence
A resource shall not automatically be treated as evidence merely because it is used in production.

### PR-010 — Resource Is Not Location
A location identifying a resource shall not be treated as the resource itself.

### PR-011 — Resource Is Not Repository
Storage or repository implementation shall not define resource identity.

### PR-012 — Resource Canonicality Requires Authority
A resource shall not be treated as canonical without applicable canonical authority.

### PR-013 — No Invented Resource
Missing or inaccessible resources shall not be silently replaced with invented canonical resources.

### PR-014 — Implementation Independence
Resource semantics shall remain independent of storage and repository implementation.

## 07.45 Canonical Statement

> **Production Resource Architecture defines the universal semantic framework for production-supporting resources, including their purpose, applicability, reuse, accessibility, traceability, maintenance, integrity, and representation boundaries. It preserves the distinction between resources, knowledge, evidence, source, location, repository, retrieval, quality, governance, and lifecycle while remaining independent of implementation technology and non-universal resource taxonomies.**
