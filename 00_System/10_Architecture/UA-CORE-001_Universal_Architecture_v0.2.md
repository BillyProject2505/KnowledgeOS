# Part I — Foundation

## 1. Purpose & Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Universal Architecture provides the Universal-level architectural model required to maintain coherence among distinct architectural concerns and their Canonical Architectural Homes.

### 1.2 Scope

It covers:

- Universal-level architectural structure;
- architectural classification;
- architectural domain placement;
- architectural boundaries;
- architectural relationships;
- architectural integration;
- architectural dependencies;
- architectural conformance;
- architectural compatibility;
- architectural evolution and lineage;
- architectural status and evidence representation.

### 1.3 Scope Exclusions

Universal Architecture does not define:

- substantive internal semantics of domain architectures;
- document semantics;
- naming and identification semantics;
- knowledge-object semantics;
- production semantics;
- governance authority;
- authorization or approval;
- organizational jurisdiction;
- project-specific architecture;
- implementation architecture;
- operational procedures.

### 1.4 Universal Governance

Universal Governance is a separate document to be developed after Universal Architecture. Universal Architecture does not assume governance authority.

## 2. Architectural Foundation

### 2.1 Architectural Coherence

Universal Architecture establishes coherence among distinct architectural concerns without collapsing substantive distinctions.

### 2.2 Architectural Separation

Distinct concerns remain distinguishable when their substantive meaning, scope, or architectural responsibility differs.

### 2.3 Canonical Architectural Home

Each substantive concern shall have an identifiable Canonical Architectural Home.

### 2.4 Universal-Level Representation

Universal Architecture represents what must be coherent at Universal level without absorbing what can remain coherently defined at a lower level.

### 2.5 Architectural Minimality

Only information necessary for Universal-level architectural coherence shall be represented.

### 2.6 Lowest Sufficient Architectural Level

Concerns shall be represented at the lowest architectural level sufficient to preserve meaning and elevated only when required for coherence.

### 2.7 Architecture, Representation, and Governance

An architectural entity, its document representation, and applicable governance authority are distinct concepts.

## 3. Normative Architectural Principles

### P-01 — Architectural Separation

Architectural concerns remain distinguishable when their substantive meaning, scope, or responsibility differs.

### P-02 — Canonical Architectural Home

Each substantive concern has an identifiable Canonical Architectural Home.

### P-03 — No Shadow Architecture

Universal Architecture shall not duplicate substantive domain architecture.

### P-04 — Integration Does Not Imply Ownership

Integration does not transfer substantive architectural responsibility.

### P-05 — Dependency Does Not Imply Hierarchy

Dependency does not establish hierarchy, subordination, or governance authority.

### P-06 — Conformance Does Not Imply Governance Authority

Conformance does not establish approval, authority, ownership, or subordination.

### P-07 — Explicit Architectural Boundaries

Architectural entities shall have explicit and interpretable boundaries.

### P-08 — Scoped Architectural Relationships

Universal relationships shall be typed and scoped according to meaning.

### P-09 — Minimum Necessary Representation

Universal Architecture shall represent only information necessary for Universal structural coherence.

### P-10 — Lowest Sufficient Architectural Level

Concerns shall remain at the lowest sufficient architectural level.

### P-11 — Architecture and Document Distinction

An architecture and its representing document remain conceptually distinct.

### P-12 — Architecture / Governance Separation

Architectural structure and evolution do not constitute governance authority.

### P-13 — Architectural Identity Continuity

Identity is preserved across change where substantive continuity remains.

### P-14 — Identity and Version Distinction

Architectural identity and version are distinct.

### P-15 — Explicit Architectural Lineage

Evolution preserves explicit and traceable lineage.

### P-16 — Historical / Current Distinction

Historical relevance does not imply current validity.

### P-17 — Evidence Does Not Establish Authority

Evidence does not by itself establish canonical status.

### P-18 — Unresolved Information Shall Remain Unresolved

Insufficient evidence shall remain explicitly unresolved.

### P-19 — Unverified Artifact Content

Existence does not establish verified substantive content.

### P-20 — Open Architectural Space

Additional architectures may be recognized when qualification criteria are met.

### P-21 — Cross-Domain Qualification

Cross-domain relationships alone do not constitute Cross-Domain Architecture.

# Part II — Architectural Model

## 4. Architectural Model

Universal Architecture models substantive architectures through Architectural Entities and their:

```text
Architectural Entity
├── Classification
├── Boundary
├── Relationship
├── Constraint
└── Change
```

Architectural Lineage is derived from identity and evolution relationships.

## 5. Architectural Classification

### 5.1 Foundational Architecture
Fundamental Universal-level architectural structure.

### 5.2 Domain Architecture
Architecture responsible for a distinct substantive domain concern.

Recognized examples:

- UDS — Universal Document Architecture;
- UNIS — Naming & Identification Architecture;
- UPKO — Production Knowledge Object Architecture;
- UPKR — Production Knowledge Registry Architecture;
- UPB — Production Architecture.

### 5.3 Cross-Domain Architecture
An independently coherent architecture whose substantive concern spans multiple domain boundaries.

### 5.4 Classification Is Not Governance Hierarchy
Classification and placement do not establish authority, hierarchy, subordination, or jurisdiction.

## 6. Architectural Domain Space

| Architecture | Principal Concern | Class | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| UDS | Document Architecture | Domain | Recognized |
| UNIS | Naming & Identification Architecture | Domain | Recognized |
| UPKO | Production Knowledge Object Architecture | Domain | Recognized |
| UPKR | Production Knowledge Registry Architecture | Domain | Open / Unverified |
| UPB | Production Architecture | Domain | Recognized |

### 6.1 Canonical Architectural Home
Identifies where substantive meaning is maintained.

### 6.2 Universal Architectural Placement
Identifies where an architectural entity is represented within the Universal architectural model.

These are distinct concepts.

### 6.3 UPKR Status
UPKR is recognized contextually, but its substantive architectural scope and internal semantics remain unresolved.

### 6.4 Domain Internal Semantics
Inclusion in Universal architectural domain space does not reproduce or supersede domain internal architecture.

## 7. Architectural Boundary Model

Architectural boundaries are first-class objects.

### 7.1 Concern Boundary
The substantive concern addressed.

### 7.2 Semantic Boundary
The semantic territory within which meaning is established.

### 7.3 Structural Boundary
The structural elements belonging within the architecture.

### 7.4 Interface Boundary
Where explicit architectural interaction occurs.

### 7.5 Exclusion Boundary
What is explicitly outside the architecture.

### 7.6 Boundary Principle
Scope describes substantive extent; boundary establishes the demarcation of inside, outside, and interaction. Overlap does not by itself create duplicate architecture or transfer responsibility.

## 8. Relationship, Integration & Dependency Model

### 8.1 Relationship Vocabulary

- contains
- composed of
- depends on
- requires
- interfaces with
- integrates with
- provides to
- consumes from
- constrains
- extends
- supersedes
- replaces

### 8.2 Dependency
Dependency indicates architectural reliance and does not imply hierarchy, subordination, responsibility transfer, or governance authority.

### 8.3 Requirement
A requirement identifies a necessary architectural condition.

### 8.4 Integration
Integration describes structural participation without transferring substantive responsibility.

### 8.5 Interface
An Architectural Interface is an explicit interaction boundary.

### 8.6 Relationship and Governance
Architectural relationships are not governance relationships unless separately established by governance architecture.

# Part III — Architectural Formalization

## 9. Architectural Object Model

### 9.1 Architectural Entity
A model-level representation of an identifiable architectural concern or architecture.

Core attributes:

- Identity
- Classification
- Scope
- Canonical Architectural Home
- Universal Architectural Placement
- Architectural Status
- Evidence metadata

### 9.2 Architectural Boundary
First-class object defining the limits of an Architectural Entity.

### 9.3 Architectural Relationship
Typed relationship between Architectural Entities.

### 9.4 Architectural Constraint
Condition limiting or guiding permissible structures or relationships.

### 9.5 Architectural Change
Object representing material architectural change and its impact.

### 9.6 Architectural Interface
Conditional object for an explicit interaction boundary or contract.

### 9.7 Architectural Lineage
Derived representation of materially related architectural states and entities across change.

## 10. Architectural Structural Schema

```text
Architectural Entity
│
├── Boundary
├── Relationship
├── Constraint
└── Change
```

Exact cardinality is not formalized unless necessary to preserve meaning.

### 10.2 Structural Minimality
The model shall not be expanded with implementation-level objects merely because an implementation could represent them.

### 10.3 Identity and Version
A version change does not automatically create a new architectural identity.

## 11. Architectural Conformance & Compatibility

### 11.1 Conformance
Satisfaction of applicable architectural requirements.

Conformance does not establish approval, authority, responsibility, or subordination.

### 11.2 Compatibility
Whether distinct architectures can interact or coexist under applicable conditions.

Compatibility is distinct from conformance, dependency, integration, and governance authority.

### 11.3 Independent Assessment
Conformance and compatibility are independently assessed properties.

## 14. External Architectural Contexts

### 14.1 Project Architecture
May conform to, depend on, interact with, or be constrained by Universal Architecture without becoming Universal Domain Architecture.

### 14.2 Implementation Architecture
Realizes or operationalizes applicable architectural requirements without becoming substantive Universal Architecture.

### 14.3 External Context Principle
External context remains conceptually distinct from Universal domain architecture.

## 15. Governance Boundary & Scope Protection

### 15.1 Architecture / Governance Separation

Universal Architecture defines:

- structure;
- classification;
- boundaries;
- relationships;
- integration;
- dependency semantics;
- conformance semantics;
- compatibility semantics;
- evolution semantics.

It does not define:

- governance authority;
- jurisdiction;
- authorization;
- approval;
- delegation;
- enforcement;
- appointment;
- governance revocation.

### 15.2 Canonical Architectural Home vs Governance Authority
Architectural Home identifies substantive architectural responsibility. Governance Authority is outside Universal Architecture.

### 15.3 Scope Protection
Universal Architecture shall not expand merely because a concern crosses domain boundaries.

### 15.4 No Shadow Architecture
Existing canonical domain architecture shall not be recreated inside Universal Architecture.

### 15.5 Cross-Domain Inflation Protection
Cross-domain interaction alone does not justify a Cross-Domain Architecture.

### 15.6 Governance Boundary
Detailed governance is deferred to the separate Universal Governance document.
