# Part I — Architectural Foundation

## 1. Purpose

Universal Architecture defines the Universal architectural model, its classification, boundaries, relationships, and evolution principles.

## 2. Scope

Universal Architecture governs the representation of substantive architectures within the Universal architectural model. It does not reproduce or supersede the internal semantics of recognized architectures.

## 3. Architectural Principles

### P-01 — Architectural Identity
Architectural identity is established independently of document identity.

### P-02 — Architectural Scope
Architecture is defined by substantive concern and boundary.

### P-03 — Architectural Boundary
Architectural boundaries establish what belongs within an architecture and what remains external.

### P-04 — Architectural Relationship
Explicit relationships shall be represented without implying hierarchy unless hierarchy is explicitly established.

### P-05 — Architectural Independence
Architectural recognition does not require structural dependence.

### P-06 — Authority Separation
Architectural classification does not establish governance authority.

### P-07 — Canonical Home
Every recognized architecture shall have a canonical architectural home.

### P-08 — Architectural Evolution
Architectural evolution shall preserve identity and traceable lineage.

### P-09 — Non-Duplication
Recognition shall not create duplicate architecture where an existing architecture already owns the substantive concern.

### P-10 — Explicit Relationship Semantics
Relationships shall use explicit semantics rather than inferred hierarchy.

### P-11 — Architectural Conformance
Conformance to an architecture does not transfer architectural ownership.

### P-12 — Boundary Preservation
Cross-architecture interaction shall preserve substantive boundaries.

### P-13 — Architectural Completeness
Recognized architecture shall have sufficient identity, scope, boundary, and relationship definition to be represented canonically.

### P-14 — Identity / Version Separation
Architectural identity and version are distinct.

### P-15 — Explicit Architectural Lineage
Evolution preserves explicit and traceable lineage.

### P-16 — Historical / Current Distinction
Historical relevance does not imply current validity.

### P-17 — Evidence Does Not Establish Authority
Evidence does not by itself establish canonical status.

### P-18 — Unresolved Information Shall Remain Unresolved
Insufficient evidence shall remain explicitly unresolved.

### P-19 — Unverified Artifact Content
Existence does not establish verified substantive content.

### P-20 — Open Architectural Space
Additional architectures may be recognized when qualification criteria are met.

### P-21 — Cross-Domain Qualification
Cross-domain relationships alone do not constitute Cross-Domain Architecture.

# Part II — Architectural Model

## 4. Architectural Model

Universal Architecture models substantive architectures through Architectural Entities and their:

```text
Architectural Entity
├── Classification
├── Boundary
├── Relationship
├── Constraint
└── Change
```

Architectural Lineage is derived from identity and evolution relationships.

## 5. Architectural Classification

### 5.1 Foundational Architecture
Fundamental Universal-level architectural structure.

### 5.2 Domain Architecture
Architecture responsible for a distinct substantive domain concern.

Recognized examples:

- UDS — Universal Document System;
- UNIS — Naming & Identification Architecture;
- UPKO — Production Knowledge Object Architecture;
- UPKR — Production Knowledge Registry Architecture;
- UPB — Production Architecture.

### 5.3 Cross-Domain Architecture
An independently coherent architecture whose substantive concern spans multiple domain boundaries.

### 5.4 Classification Is Not Governance Hierarchy
Classification and placement do not establish authority, hierarchy, subordination, or jurisdiction.

## 6. Architectural Domain Space

| Architecture | Principal Concern | Class | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| UDS | Document System | Domain | Recognized |
| UNIS | Naming & Identification Architecture | Domain | Recognized |
| UPKO | Production Knowledge Object Architecture | Domain | Recognized |
| UPKR | Production Knowledge Registry Architecture | Domain | Open / Unverified |
| UPB | Production Architecture | Domain | Recognized |

### 6.1 Canonical Architectural Home
Identifies where substantive meaning is maintained.

### 6.2 Universal Architectural Placement
Identifies where an architectural entity is represented within the Universal architectural model.

These are distinct concepts.

### 6.3 UPKR Status
UPKR is recognized contextually, but its substantive architectural scope and internal semantics remain unresolved.

### 6.4 Domain Internal Semantics
Inclusion in Universal architectural domain space does not reproduce or supersede domain internal architecture.

## 7. Architectural Boundary Model

Architectural boundaries are first-class objects.

### 7.1 Concern Boundary
The substantive concern addressed.

### 7.2 Semantic Boundary
The semantic territory within which meaning is established.

### 7.3 Structural Boundary
The structural elements belonging within the architecture.

### 7.4 Interface Boundary
Where explicit architectural interaction occurs.

### 7.5 Exclusion Boundary
What is explicitly outside the architecture.

### 7.6 Boundary Principle
Scope describes substantive extent; boundary establishes the demarcation of inside, outside, and interaction. Overlap does not by itself create duplicate architecture or transfer responsibility.

## 8. Relationship, Integration & Dependency Model

### 8.1 Relationship Vocabulary

- contains
- composed of
- depends on
- requires
- interfaces with
- integrates with
- provides to
- consumes from
- constrains
- extends

### 8.2 Relationship Semantics
Relationships shall be interpreted according to their declared semantics and shall not be converted into hierarchy by implication.

### 8.3 UDS–UNIS Relationship
UDS and UNIS are recognized as peer Domain Architectures within Universal Architecture. UDS may depend on, conform to, or interface with applicable Universal naming and identification architecture established by UNIS, but such relationship does not transfer architectural ownership or governance authority from UDS to UNIS.

### 8.4 UDS–UNIR Relationship
Where UDS requires Universal naming or identification registry state, it may interface with UNIR in accordance with applicable UNIS and UNIR authority boundaries. Such interaction does not make UNIR the architectural owner of UDS.

### 8.5 Relationship Does Not Establish Subordination
A dependency, conformance, interface, or integration relationship does not by itself establish architectural hierarchy, authority, or subordination.

## 9. Architectural Constraint Model

Architectures may define constraints on their own internal scope and on explicit interfaces with other architectures.

## 10. Architectural Change Model

Architectural change shall preserve identity, lineage, boundary, and explicit relationship semantics.

# Part III — Architectural Recognition

## 11. Recognition Criteria

An architecture may be recognized when it has:

- distinct substantive concern;
- explicit identity;
- defined scope;
- defined boundaries;
- sufficient internal coherence;
- explicit architectural relationships;
- identifiable canonical home.

## 12. Recognition Status

Recognition status may be:

- Recognized
- Open / Unverified
- Historical
- Superseded
- Rejected

Recognition status does not itself establish governance authority.

---

# Part IV — Architectural Governance Boundary

## 13. Authority Boundary

Universal Architecture defines architectural classification, placement, boundaries, and relationship semantics. It does not replace the governance authority of a recognized architecture over its substantive internal semantics.

## 14. Architectural Ownership

Each recognized architecture retains ownership of its substantive architectural semantics unless an explicit higher-level governance instrument establishes otherwise.

## 15. Cross-Architecture Coordination

Cross-architecture coordination shall be expressed through explicit relationships and boundary definitions rather than implicit authority.
