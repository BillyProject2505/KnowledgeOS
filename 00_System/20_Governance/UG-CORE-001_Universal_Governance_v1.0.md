## 0. Document Foundation

### 0.1 Purpose

Universal Governance defines the governance framework through which the Universal system is directed, controlled, maintained, evolved, and kept conformant with its established architectural foundation.

Universal Governance exists as a distinct canonical document from Universal Architecture.

Universal Architecture establishes the structural foundation of the Universal system. Universal Governance establishes the authority, rules, decision mechanisms, controls, responsibilities, lifecycle mechanisms, and change processes through which that architecture and the systems built upon it are governed.

Universal Governance shall therefore govern the **operation and evolution of the Universal system** without redefining the architectural model established by Universal Architecture.

### 0.2 Foundational Relationship

The relationship between the two documents is:

**Universal Architecture → defines the structural foundation.**

**Universal Governance → governs the authority, control, evolution, and conformance of that foundation and the Universal system operating upon it.**

Universal Governance shall not absorb architectural definitions merely because those definitions are relevant to governance.

Likewise, Universal Architecture shall not be expanded to contain governance mechanisms merely because those mechanisms operate upon architectural structures.

### 0.3 Governance Objective

Universal Governance shall ensure that the Universal system:

1. remains internally coherent;
2. preserves its canonical foundations;
3. has explicit authority for decisions and changes;
4. maintains controlled evolution;
5. prevents unauthorized semantic or structural drift;
6. establishes clear ownership and decision rights;
7. provides mechanisms for conformance and compliance;
8. preserves historical and decision traceability;
9. protects boundaries between Universal-level and domain-level authority; and
10. provides a stable governance foundation for all subordinate Universal systems, standards, registries, architectures, and production frameworks.

### 0.4 Governance Scope

Universal Governance governs Universal-level concerns including:

- governance authority;
- governance principles;
- decision rights;
- authority hierarchy;
- responsibility and accountability;
- canonicality;
- change governance;
- amendment and revision control;
- lifecycle governance;
- conformance governance;
- exception governance;
- conflict resolution;
- dependency and boundary governance;
- registry authority;
- canonical publication;
- supersession and retirement;
- governance evidence and traceability;
- architectural evolution governance; and
- interaction between Universal-level governance and subordinate domain governance.

Universal Governance does **not** replace the governance mechanisms of individual domains.

Domain-specific governance may exist beneath the Universal Governance framework, provided that it does not contradict or override applicable Universal-level authority.

## 1. Governance Principles

### 1.1 Architectural Primacy

Universal Architecture is the established structural foundation of the Universal system.

Governance decisions shall operate against that foundation and shall not silently redefine it.

Any governance decision that requires an architectural change shall identify that architectural dependency explicitly and follow the applicable architectural change process.

### 1.2 Separation of Architecture and Governance

Architecture and governance are distinct normative concerns.

Architecture answers:

> **What is the Universal system and how is it structurally organized?**

Governance answers:

> **Who has authority over it, according to which rules, through which decisions, and under what controls?**

Neither layer shall be used as an implicit substitute for the other.

### 1.3 Explicit Authority

Every normative Universal decision shall have an identifiable source of authority.

No document, registry, standard, process, or operational practice shall become Universal-level authority merely through repeated use or informal acceptance.

Authority shall be established through an explicit governance mechanism.

### 1.4 Canonicality by Authorization

A document or artifact becomes canonical through an explicit authorization process.

Existence, publication, repository presence, implementation, or historical usage alone does not establish canonical authority.

### 1.5 Controlled Evolution

The Universal system shall be capable of evolving without losing structural continuity.

Changes shall therefore be evaluated for:

- scope;
- authority;
- dependencies;
- impact;
- compatibility;
- conformance;
- migration requirements;
- supersession implications; and
- historical traceability.

### 1.6 No Silent Semantic Drift

A change that materially alters the meaning, authority, boundary, relationship, lifecycle, or interpretation of an existing canonical object shall not be treated as a merely editorial change.

Material semantic change shall require the appropriate governance treatment.

### 1.7 Traceable Decisions

Material governance decisions shall remain traceable to:

- the authority that made the decision;
- the object or subject affected;
- the decision itself;
- its rationale where required;
- its effective state;
- applicable dependencies; and
- resulting artifacts or changes.

### 1.8 Boundary Preservation

Universal Governance shall preserve explicit boundaries between:

- Universal-level authority;
- Universal Architecture;
- Universal standards;
- Universal registries;
- domain architectures;
- domain governance;
- production systems;
- implementation artifacts; and
- operational practices.

A lower-level artifact shall not acquire higher-level authority merely by reference.

### 1.9 Temporal Integrity

Universal authority shall be interpreted according to its applicable temporal state.

Current authority, historical authority, superseded authority, withdrawn authority, and deprecated material shall be distinguishable.

Historical artifacts may remain valuable as evidence or historical reference without remaining normative authority.

### 1.10 Governance Before Materialization

Where a materialization, migration, transfer, retirement, supersession, or structural change would alter Universal-level authority, the governing decision shall precede the assumption that such change has occurred.

Implementation shall not be treated as authorization.

## 2. Governance Authority Model

### 2.1 Purpose

The Universal Governance Authority Model defines how authority is established, exercised, delegated, constrained, recorded, and reviewed across the Universal system.

Its purpose is to prevent ambiguity regarding who or what may:

- establish normative authority;
- approve canonical artifacts;
- authorize structural or semantic change;
- establish or modify Universal-level standards;
- establish registries and registry authority;
- approve exceptions;
- resolve conflicts;
- authorize supersession or retirement; and
- determine conformance requirements.

Authority shall be explicit, bounded, and traceable.

### 2.2 Authority Is Not Equivalent to Existence

The presence of an artifact within the Universal ecosystem does not by itself establish governance authority.

The following shall not independently constitute Universal authority:

- repository presence;
- file creation;
- publication without authorization;
- implementation;
- operational usage;
- historical adoption;
- repeated citation;
- informal approval;
- individual preference; or
- derivative materialization.

Normative authority must be established through an applicable governance mechanism.

### 2.3 Authority Levels

Universal Governance recognizes the following authority levels.

#### Level 0 — Foundational Authority

Foundational Authority consists of the highest-order principles and structures that establish the identity and constitutional boundaries of the Universal system.

It establishes the conditions within which all subordinate Universal governance operates.

Foundational Authority shall not be modified through ordinary document revision.

Any change affecting foundational authority requires explicit constitutional-level governance treatment.

#### Level 1 — Universal Governance Authority

Universal Governance Authority establishes the rules by which the Universal system is governed.

It includes:

- governance principles;
- authority allocation;
- decision rights;
- change governance;
- canonicality rules;
- conformance mechanisms; and
- exception mechanisms.

Universal Governance is the principal normative instrument for these concerns.

#### Level 2 — Universal Architectural Authority

Universal Architectural Authority establishes the structural architecture of the Universal system.

Universal Architecture is the principal authority at this level.

Governance controls the evolution of this authority but does not replace it.

#### Level 3 — Universal Standard Authority

Universal Standards establish normative rules for specific Universal capabilities, concepts, naming systems, identification systems, lifecycle systems, registries, or other standardized concerns.

A Universal Standard shall operate within the authority established by Universal Governance and the structural boundaries established by Universal Architecture.

#### Level 4 — Registry Authority

A Universal Registry establishes authoritative records for the objects within its defined registry scope.

Registry authority is limited to the registry's declared ownership and semantic scope.

A registry shall not independently redefine concepts owned by a higher-level authority.

#### Level 5 — Domain Authority

Domain Authority governs a specific Universal domain, system, production environment, or specialized body of work.

Domain Authority may establish domain-specific rules where Universal-level authority has not reserved the matter and where those rules do not contradict applicable Universal authority.

#### Level 6 — Implementation Authority

Implementation artifacts translate normative requirements into executable, operational, or production-specific forms.

Implementation authority does not independently modify normative meaning.

#### Level 7 — Operational Practice

Operational Practice consists of actual usage, procedures, habits, conventions, and implementation behavior.

Operational practice may provide evidence for governance review but does not automatically become normative authority.

### 2.4 Authority Precedence

Where authorities overlap, the following precedence applies unless an explicit governing instrument establishes a narrower exception:

**Foundational Authority → Universal Governance Authority → Universal Architectural Authority → Universal Standard Authority → Registry Authority → Domain Authority → Implementation Authority → Operational Practice**

Lower-level authority shall conform to applicable higher-level authority.

A lower-level artifact cannot override a higher-level normative requirement unless an explicit higher-level mechanism authorizes such effect.
